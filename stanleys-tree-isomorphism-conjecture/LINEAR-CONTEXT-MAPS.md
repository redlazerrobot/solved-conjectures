# Formal linear ambiguities for several attached trees

The [edge-context inversion](EDGE-CONTEXTS.md) uses that the unknown coefficients actually come from a rooted tree. If they are treated as arbitrary homogeneous polynomials, the inverse problem has an exact linear threshold. This separates a limitation of the formal equations from a possible collision of trees.

Fix k known rooted contexts of distinct positive orders s_1<…<s_k. Use the polynomial J_r from the edge-context note. Put M_(j,r)=J_r(S_j) for r=1,…,n. Give input coordinate r weight shift r: its coefficient is homogeneous of weight n−r and the j-th output has weight n+s_j. Over Q the first order n with a nonzero formal kernel is

    n_0 = Σ_j s_j + (k+1)(k+2)/2.

At equality the dimension of the homogeneous kernel is one. In particular the shortest path contexts of orders 1 through k give this linear recovery for n<(k+1)^2. The two-context connected-set formula distinguishes actual rooted trees even where the formal kernel is nonzero.

## Proof

Work over the polynomial ring in variables u_i through i=s_k+n. Each entry is monic in u_(s_j+r) and uses only lower indices otherwise. Every square minor with ordered row and column sets is nonzero: only the bottom-right entry contains the variable of largest index, and its coefficient in the determinant is the smaller minor obtained by removing that row and column. This also proves injectivity when n≤k.

For n>k, the rank-defect locus of M has codimension at least n−k+1. To see this, pass to an algebraic closure and introduce a nonzero projective row vector λ annihilating all n columns. Stratify by its last nonzero entry j, normalize that entry to 1, and set all subsequent ones to zero. In increasing column order r, the resulting equation is monic linear in u_(s_j+r) with only lower variables otherwise. The n equations successively eliminate n distinct variables, for j−1 free λ parameters. The incidence stratum therefore has dimension at most (s_k+n)−n+j−1. The projection of the union contains every rank-deficient point and has dimension at most (s_k+n)−n+k−1. Polynomial rings are Cohen–Macaulay, so the maximal-minor ideal has the required grade at least n−k+1. The height computation is unchanged by the base-field extension.

By the Buchsbaum–Rim exactness theorem, the kernel of M is generated by the cofactor vectors on k+1 columns: the entry on each chosen column is, up to alternating sign, the k×k minor on the other chosen columns. See [Buchsbaum and Rim, 1964, Corollary 2.6](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1964-0159860-7) and [Kreuzer, Migliore, Nagel and Peterson, Fact 2.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/alg-geom/9708021). For a chosen set I of columns the shifted degree of this vector is Σ_j s_j+Σ_(r in I)r. All these vectors are nonzero by the minor argument. Their least possible degree is n_0, attained only for I={1,…,k+1}. This proves both threshold assertions. Every component of a cofactor vector is a sum of products of k polynomials without constant terms. Thus, at any order, no formal ambiguity affects an entry with fewer than k off-root component variables.

The [exact rational rank check](verify_context_minors.py) constructs M from context edge cuts for paths of orders 1 and 2 and expands on the monomial bases. SymPy computed ranks 30, 45, 66 and 95 on 30, 45, 67 and 97 columns respectively for n=7,8,9,10. The formal rank first drops at nine, in agreement with the proof. For k=1 the first ambiguity is realized by actual trees. For k≥2 the two distinct rooted contexts already distinguish every pair of actual rooted trees by the nonlinear formula in the preceding note. Publication priority for this application of the established algebra is undetermined.
