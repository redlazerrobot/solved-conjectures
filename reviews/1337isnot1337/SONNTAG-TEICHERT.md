# Sonntag–Teichert conjecture

Audit of the [unrefereed proof credited to the Cinna Davis and GPT-6.2 Astra collection](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/3e0c5aaf3be0e6b9e092da1558f87ea09268799f/results/sonntag-teichert/PROOF.md), pinned at `3e0c5aaf3be0e6b9e092da1558f87ea09268799f`. The original problem is due to Martin Sonntag and Hanns-Martin Teichert, [Opuscula Mathematica 30 (2010), p. 182](https://doi.org/10.7494/OpMath.2010.30.2.179). This review makes no publication-priority claim.

## Scope of the written argument

In a finite nonempty tournament, the hyperedges are all inclusion-minimal vertex sets that dominate outward. There is at most one nontrivial connected component. The paper treats an isolated vertex and a vertex incident only to its singleton edge as trivial. For a finite oriented graph allowing missing arcs but excluding opposite arcs, the pinned note further shows that disconnected support consists of exactly two vertex-disjoint stars, including single-edge stars. Every pair of such stars is realizable, with arbitrarily many vertices outside the support. The 2010 paper already reported tournament checks through order nine.

Proof review. Let C be the minimal closed in-neighbourhoods and D the minimal dominating sets. Domination is exactly hitting C. Conversely a set missing any containment of a closed in-neighbourhood has a dominating complement. Thus C is the family of minimal transversals of D. If D splits into two nonempty edge families on disjoint supports, their respective minimal transversals combine freely to form C. A universal source would be mandatory in every dominating set and give just one minimal dominating set, so no singleton dominates in the split case. Hence each side has at least two transversals: a unique transversal on one side would give a vertex common to every member of C.

For p and q side transversals there are pq distinct minimal closed in-neighbourhoods. Pick their representatives. Each lies in its own row transversal or its own column transversal. Two representatives from the same row lying in its row transversal would require opposite arcs; the same holds for a column. Thus pq ≤ p+q, forcing p=q=2. The two row transversals are disjoint, since a shared vertex dominates by itself. The columns likewise are disjoint. This produces disjoint diagonal closed in-neighbourhoods, impossible in a tournament because the tail of the arc between their representatives lies in both. The argument applies at arbitrary finite order.

For the oriented extension, the four disjoint parts yield two complete bipartite components by transversal duality. Equality in the counting bound places exactly one row representative in each row and one column representative in each column. A nonrepresentative in a row part sends arcs to both representatives that contain its part in their neighbourhoods. Its own neighbourhood must contain the unique diagonal cell excluding those two representatives. Nonrepresentatives in both row parts would therefore require opposite arcs. The same holds for the two column parts. This gives a singleton part in each bipartite component. Inspection of the pinned construction gives the converse: the four chosen closed in-neighbourhoods occur exactly, and all further neighbourhoods contain one of them. Within each star the singleton part prevents opposite-arc conflicts. No gap found in this written argument.

## Executed checks

Python 3.12.14, assertions enabled. From a checkout at the pin, the upstream command is `python3 results/sonntag-teichert/verify.py`. We ran the file with Git blob ID `235c37b343f9114b4224ab5c54522500eb1c85d4`. Its output reported 33,867 labelled tournaments through order six, 59,809 labelled oriented graphs through order five (396 with disconnected support), and 49 two-star constructions with an isolated vertex.

Run the separately implemented [checker](verify_sonntag.py) with `python3 reviews/1337isnot1337/verify_sonntag.py`. It tests domination from the arc definition, prunes supersets of smaller dominating sets, and traverses hyperedge intersections. Across the same 33,867 tournaments it found zero disconnected supports. In the oriented case all 396 disconnected supports were pairs of stars, six at order four and 390 at order five. These are finite checks of the definitions. Neither local program proves the unrestricted statements.
